Hi, I’m Hind, an engineer and applied AI researcher. While many enter AI through computer science, my path began in electrical engineering. I started my career designing satellite communication hardware for aviation, gaining hands-on experience with RF circuitry, power-supply design, hardware validation, and system integration. Working so closely with physical systems, and the challenges of their reliability and performance, sparked my interest in how data and intelligence could be used to better understand and optimize complex infrastructure.

That curiosity led me back to graduate school, where I completed both a Master’s and a PhD in Electrical and Computer Engineering. My doctoral research focused on AI-enabled communication systems, exploring how deep learning and reinforcement learning can improve network performance in dynamic, real-world environments. This included developing approaches for predicting connectivity performance and optimizing sequential decisions such as handovers in low-Earth-orbit satellite networks.

Professionally, my career has evolved from hardware engineering to data science and, most recently, machine learning engineering. Today, I develop AI-driven systems for monitoring, predicting, and diagnosing the performance of airborne connectivity systems. My work brings together large-scale aircraft and network telemetry, machine learning, domain knowledge, and production software to help engineering and support teams identify anomalous behaviour, investigate system failures, and optimize performance. My background allows me to work across both the data and the physical systems generating it.

Across more than seven years in industry, I have learned to value AI systems that are not only technically sophisticated, but also reliable, interpretable, and useful in practice. I am particularly interested in machine learning for infrastructure, dynamic systems, and real-world control and optimization. 

Outside of work and research, I enjoy travelling and staying active. I am also deeply committed to community and knowledge-sharing. I engage with the broader technology community through groups such as Google Developer Groups, volunteer at community events, and share my work through academic publications and conference presentations. For me, impact extends beyond building and deploying technology, it also includes making knowledge accessible, supporting others, and contributing to the communities around me.

If you’re interested in applied AI, machine learning, or the intersection of research, engineering, and real-world systems, I’d love to connect.
